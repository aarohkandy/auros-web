This is the category we would check first, and it is the one on this list that can make the
answer no.

A pupil with an access arrangement depends on a specific tool behaving a specific way. That is
not a preference and it is not a nice-to-have. If the reading support that a child has used for
three years does not come across, that is a serious outcome and the right answer may be that
those particular machines do not migrate.

Many of these products have moved to the browser in the last few years, and where yours has, it
comes across with no effort, because it is a website. Where it has not, treat it the way you
would treat the monitoring agent: get it in writing before you commit.

Tell us which of these matter and we will look each one up with you in the public compatibility
database before you have spent anything. That is a reading of somebody else's evidence, not a
test we have run. For a tool a child depends on daily, a reading is not enough on its own, and
the answer you want in writing is the supplier's, not ours.
